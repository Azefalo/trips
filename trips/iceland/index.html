The trip

Iceland was never really a question — it was more of an inevitability. The kind of place that sits in the back of your mind for years until one day you just book the flight and figure the rest out along the way.

We drove the full Ring Road (Route 1) over 10 days, starting and ending in Reykjavík. The map on the left shows every stop — from Seljalandsfoss and Skógafoss to the Jökulsárlón glacier lagoon and the Snæfellsnes peninsula. Open it on your phone and follow along.

Winter/early spring is underrated here. Crowds are smaller, the Northern Lights are still visible, and the low golden light makes every waterfall look like it belongs in a painting.

Itinerary

Day by day along the Ring Road.

Day 01

Reykjavík

Arrived at Keflavík International Airport early morning. Picked up the rental car (booked in advance — 4WD is essential in winter) and drove straight to Reykjavík, 45 minutes away.

  • Hallgrímskirkja church — climb the tower for the best city view (small fee)
  • Laugavegur street for lunch and coffee
  • Sunset walk along Sæbraut by the sea
★ Book 4WD weeks in advance — rental companies run out fast in winter. We used Sad Cars (surprisingly great value).
Day 02

Golden Circle

The classic day-trip circuit from Reykjavík — about 300km and perfectly doable in a day. Starts with Þingvellir (Thingvellir) National Park, then the Geysir geothermal area, and finishes at Gullfoss waterfall.

  • Þingvellir — walk between the North American and Eurasian tectonic plates
  • Strokkur geyser erupts every 5–7 minutes — stay for 2–3 cycles to get the shot
  • Gullfoss — impressive even in winter, when the mist freezes on the surrounding rocks
★ Start early (leave Reykjavík by 8am) to beat the tour buses at each stop. The Golden Circle is popular for a reason.
Day 03

South Coast — Waterfalls

The South Coast is where Iceland truly delivers. This stretch between Reykjavík and Vík is non-stop: waterfalls you can walk behind, black cliffs, and dramatic basalt columns.

  • Seljalandsfoss — waterfall you can walk around and behind (get wet, worth it)
  • Gljúfrabúi — hidden waterfall 5 min walk from Seljalandsfoss, almost nobody goes
  • Skógafoss — massive, thundering, rainbow in the morning spray
  • Dyrhólaey arch and black sand beach (Reynisfjara)
★ Reynisfjara black sand beach has sneaker waves — stay back from the water even when it looks calm. People have died here.
Day 04

Jökulsárlón — Glacier Lagoon

A long driving day (about 3.5h from Vík) but the destination makes every kilometre worth it. Jökulsárlón is a glacial lake filled with floating icebergs calved from the Vatnajökull glacier — the largest in Europe.

  • Jökulsárlón lagoon — icebergs drift from the glacier to the ocean, some crystal-blue inside
  • Diamond Beach — just across the road, where ice chunks wash up on black sand
  • Skaftafell — good short hike (2–3h) to Svartifoss waterfall (basalt columns)
★ Get to Diamond Beach at sunrise — the light on the ice is unreal and you'll likely have it to yourself.
Day 05

East Fjords

The East Fjords are Iceland's quiet side — winding coastal roads, tiny fishing villages, and very few tourists. It's a long drive but meditative. The kind of Iceland that doesn't end up on Instagram.

  • Djúpivogur fishing village — lunch stop, excellent fish soup
  • Hengifoss waterfall — striking red clay stripes in the canyon walls (2h hike round trip)
  • Egilsstaðir — largest town in the east, good base for the night
★ Fill up on petrol whenever you see a station in the fjords — gaps between stations can be 100km+.
Days 06–07

North — Mývatn & Akureyri

The north of Iceland is geothermally wild — boiling mud pots, sulphur vents, volcanic craters, and a lake teeming with birdlife. Akureyri is the northern capital, worth an afternoon wander.

  • Mývatn Nature Baths — think Blue Lagoon without the crowds, a quarter of the price
  • Námafjall Hverir — steaming, gurgling, otherworldly sulphur fields
  • Dimmuborgir — lava field with bizarre rock formations (Game of Thrones filmed here)
  • Goðafoss waterfall — the "waterfall of the gods," wide and beautiful
★ Mývatn Nature Baths: book online and go early morning or evening to avoid tour groups. Far better vibe than the Blue Lagoon.
Days 08–10

Snæfellsnes & Return

The Snæfellsnes peninsula is often called "Iceland in miniature" — glacier, lava fields, fishing villages, dramatic coastline. Jules Verne set the entrance to his "Journey to the Centre of the Earth" here. Easy to see why.

  • Snæfellsjökull glacier — the glacier Jules Verne made famous, hike or snowmobile
  • Arnarstapi — dramatic basalt arch sea cliffs and colony of Arctic terns
  • Búðir black church — isolated, photogenic, a 5-minute stop that stays with you
  • Blue Lagoon (on the way to the airport, day 10) — book 2–3 months ahead
★ If Northern Lights are on your list, check the aurora forecast (en.vedur.is) every night. Clear skies + KP index ≥ 3 = good chance.

Practical tips

✈️

Getting there

Fly into Keflavík (KEF), 45 min from Reykjavík. Many European airlines offer direct routes. Icelandair often has good fares with a free stopover option.

🚗

Getting around

Rent a 4WD — mandatory in winter, strongly recommended year-round. The Ring Road (Route 1) is paved. Mountain roads (F-roads) require a true 4WD and are closed in winter. Check road.is daily.

🏨

Where to sleep

Accommodation books out fast. Plan and book in advance, especially for summer. Guesthouses and farm stays (called "Farmhouse Hotels") are charming and often cheaper than hotels. Camping is popular May–September.

💰

Budget

Iceland is expensive. Budget roughly €150–200/day per person for accommodation, car, food, and fuel. Cook your own meals where possible — supermarkets (Bónus, Krónan) are everywhere and save a lot. Eating out is very costly.

🌤️

Best season

Summer (Jun–Aug) = midnight sun, green landscapes, all F-roads open. Winter (Nov–Mar) = Northern Lights, snow, fewer crowds. Spring and autumn are sweet spots — decent weather, lower prices, still a chance at the aurora.

🎒

What to pack

Waterproof everything — jacket, boots, trousers. Layers are key. Weather changes in minutes. Sunscreen in summer (midnight sun burns). Aurora alarm app if you're chasing the lights. Portable charger — you'll use your phone as GPS constantly.
