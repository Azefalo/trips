The trip

It was me and four friends sitting by the Garonne in Toulouse, after a really fun day, but with a goodbye feeling in the air — two of them were moving away, and that was our last night living in the same city. In the middle of that emotion, someone said "let's plan our next trip together so we see each other again". That's one of the things I love most: being around friends, planning adventures together, being spontaneous.

We started throwing places out there. We were thinking big — see the Northern Lights, head up to northern Europe, maybe Finland, until someone said Iceland and everyone got excited about the landscapes and the incredible things you can do there. We actually started looking at flights and found a Transavia route Paris–Reykjavík every Monday at 30% of the usual price. In the middle of all that excitement we transferred the money and bought the tickets right there for a trip 3–4 months later. That's how it all started.

Then we started researching what we'd do, what we'd visit, who else to invite. The best option was renting a car so we could build our own route at our own pace — until it got to the point where one car wouldn't fit everyone and we were looking at two. We checked accommodations and even hostels were too expensive to share a room, until we found out we could rent a camper van from Rent.is (the cheapest company we found, and with impeccable service). It was easily the best idea — the van became our temporary home, we drove kilometre after kilometre seeing the country and slept inside it every night.

After a lot of research and planning we put together a small itinerary: things to visit, where we could park the van (only at campsites), where to use the bathroom, shower, cook, refuel. I built the map you see on the side with all those references — we didn't make it to everything, but the most important and worthwhile stops are there, plus options of what to do. Below I'll detail each day of our trip and then there's my Polarsteps log, an app that shows the whole route we did, where I dropped photos of the places and the things we lived. I hope this guide is useful to remind me of these memories in the future, but also useful for anyone who wants to discover this wonderful country and have a real route to follow.

Itinerary

Day by day along the Ring Road.

Day 01

Reykjavík — Arrival

We arrived late at night because of a flight delay. There was a transfer at the airport that took us to pick up the van at Rent.is — I had already booked and paid everything online, two Renault Master 5-seaters. A historic snowstorm had just hit. We grabbed the van, picked the others back up at the airport and drove straight to the Reykjavík campsite. Luckily we'd got a voucher and bought sandwiches in Paris during the delay, because everything was already closed by the time we arrived.

  • Airport transfer + van pickup at Rent.is
  • Reykjavík Campsite — first night
  • The campsite has a community food shelf — leftover food (biscuits, coffee, tea, tuna, pasta) that people leave when they're heading out so others can take it instead of throwing it away. Saved us for a snack and breakfast the next day.
★ Always bring snacks with you on the flight — most shops and supermarkets in Iceland close early and you'll be glad you did.
Day 02

Reykjavík → Vík

We woke up and went straight to Hallgrímskirkja, a Protestant church with stunning rocket-shaped architecture. After that we hit Bónus, the cheapest supermarket in the country (the one with the pink piggy logo). We bought lots of water, biscuits, snacks, and food to cook for lunch and dinner — our routine became sandwiches at lunchtime and a proper cooked dinner at the campsite. Then we drove south to Seljalandsfoss, one of Iceland's most famous waterfalls — and I get why. After that, Skógafoss, a massive waterfall with a little trail that climbs up to more beautiful falls upriver. We finished at Vík Campsite — cooked, showered, slept.

  • Hallgrímskirkja church
  • Bónus supermarket stock-up
  • Seljalandsfoss waterfall
  • Skógafoss waterfall + the upriver trail
  • Vík Campsite
★ Buy all your food at Bónus — easily the cheapest supermarket in Iceland. Stock up on water, snacks and lunch/dinner ingredients.
Day 03

Vík → Skaftafell (van stuck + aurora)

We arrived at the campsite at night, but waking up at sunrise with that landscape — the mountains, the snow, the orange sun — was magical. Since we hadn't had time to see everything the day before, we backtracked to Kvernufoss, a waterfall tucked inside a small canyon, gorgeous. Then to the beach at Hálsanefshellir Cave, famous for its perfectly hexagonal basalt columns and black volcanic "sand". The view there is unreal. We stopped at Vík í Mýrdal Church for a top-down view of the town.

Because of the snowstorm, sun heat and freezing wind, almost every road in the south was dark blue on safetravel.is (which means black ice). Between the wind and the speed of the van we ended up sliding off the road. We were stuck for hours — tried pushing the van, anything, but it was too heavy. A local stopped his truck and gave us the contact of a tow company. They came with a 4x4 and managed (with difficulty) to pull the van out, plus a few other cars they found along the way. We carried on, more carefully, to Fjaðrárgljúfur, a canyon completely covered in snow — stunning.

Because of the stuck-van delay, we had to drive at night to reach the next campsite. The aurora app showed a high index (KP=4) and the area we were in had clear skies. Driving while seeing the aurora dancing in the sky, watching the colours move — incredible. We pulled into a few roadside parkings to take it in. And then, after we made it to Skaftafell Campground, we were hit by an even stronger aurora (KP=5) — bright green with a hint of red, visible to the naked eye, dancing right on top of the van. Unbelievable.

  • Kvernufoss waterfall (hidden canyon)
  • Hálsanefshellir Cave + Reynisfjara black sand beach
  • Vík í Mýrdal Church viewpoint
  • Van slid off the road — rescued by a local tow company
  • Fjaðrárgljúfur canyon (snow-covered)
  • Skaftafell Campground — surprise KP=5 aurora overhead
★ Always check safetravel.is before driving. Dark blue = black ice. Save a local tow contact in your phone before you leave the campsite.
Day 04

Jökulsárlón → Egilsstaðir

I'd booked an ice cave tour at the Jökulsárlón Glacier through getyourguide.com. Unfortunately, with bad weather, poor visibility and the van slipping on icy roads, we missed it. A 40-minute drive took us 1h40. We got a full refund (a lot of tours were cancelled that day anyway). We still got to enjoy it though — it's a genuinely beautiful place where you watch icebergs being pushed out to sea. Right across the road is the famous Diamond Beach, where those ice chunks wash up on black sand (best in the morning). We took loads of photos and warmed up with hot chocolate.

After lunch we headed north. The snowy landscapes, the mountains, the sea, the company — all of it was special. The drive took hours because of road conditions and visibility, but we made it to Camp Egilsstaðir. We cooked dinner and planned the next day. The forecast was for absurdly strong winds — 60 m/s by 7am in some spots, and 140 m/s in others. Not km/h. Metres per second. We talked it through with another couple at the campsite and had to decide: keep going around the ring, or wait here a day for the wind to pass and head back south.

  • Jökulsárlón glacier lagoon — icebergs drifting to the sea
  • Diamond Beach (best in the morning)
  • Refund on the ice cave tour due to weather
  • Long, snowy drive to Camp Egilsstaðir
  • Forecast: hurricane-force winds the next morning
★ Book tours through GetYourGuide — they give you a real refund when weather cancels things. Crucial in Iceland where weather changes hourly.
Day 05

North — Mývatn & Akureyri

We decided to leave at 2am heading northwest, continuing the ring. I'll admit I was a little scared — we were in a much more isolated area and had no idea how long a tow would take if anything went wrong. The wind on the road was brutal, you could literally feel the van being pushed sideways through the steering wheel. But thank God it worked out — we made it through the worst stretch.

Then on to Hverfjall, an inactive volcano that left a giant crater behind. We had the idea of sliding down the snowy path on the way back and it was so much fun. Then the Blue Lake, a warm volcanic-water lake with so many minerals it turns brilliant blue. Then Hverir, a geothermal park next to the old volcano — boiling water, sulphur smell, reddish-orange earth and steam everywhere. It felt like being on Mars. After that, Goðafoss — yet another famous, ridiculously beautiful waterfall. This is also where we found out we got the tour refund.

Then Akureyri, Iceland's second city — famous for whale meat and fishing. We ate the typical Icelandic hot dog. To wind down, we went to one of Iceland's lagoons — Forest Lagoon, a resort/spa with naturally heated pools, this one literally set inside a forest. What a place. We stayed 3–4 hours. Slept at Camping Hamrar.

  • Hverfjall volcano crater (slide down the snow on the way back)
  • Blue Lake (geothermal mineral lake)
  • Hverir geothermal park (sulphur, steam, Mars vibes)
  • Goðafoss waterfall
  • Akureyri + typical Icelandic hot dog
  • Forest Lagoon — 3–4 hours in heated forest pools
  • Camping Hamrar
★ If you have to drive in extreme weather, do it at night/early morning when wind tends to drop. And check the forecast in m/s, not km/h — Iceland measures it differently and 30 m/s is already terrifying.
Day 06

Reykjafoss → Troll Garden

We woke up and drove to Reykjafoss — my favourite place of the whole trip. A gorgeous waterfall, lots of photos. You can rent horses at the parking lot for a ride, but the coolest part is a hot spring next to it that they turned into a small pool called Fosslaug geothermal pool. 6°C outside, and we jumped in — so much fun.

Then we drove down towards Reykjavík through unreal landscapes — fields full of sheep, snowless mountains, everything golden from dry grass and sunlight. We stopped at Hraunfossar Waterfall, a park with multiple cascades and incredibly blue water. One friend really wanted to see the troll park, so we went at night and found one of the best campsites of the trip: Troll Garden Camping — jacuzzi, great kitchen, the lot. Slept there.

  • Reykjafoss waterfall (my favourite spot of the trip)
  • Fosslaug geothermal pool — bathe at 6°C outside
  • Hraunfossar Waterfall — blue water cascades
  • Troll Garden Camping (jacuzzi + amazing kitchen)
★ Don't skip the smaller waterfalls — Reykjafoss and the geothermal pool right next to it ended up being more memorable than any of the famous ones.
Day 07

Golden Circle

We visited Troll Garden before sunrise and then headed for the famous Golden Circle — Iceland's classic circuit. We saved it for last because of the weather, and got lucky with an absolutely stunning clear day, completely different from the rest of the trip. First stop: Mosfellsbær, to take in the fields and mountain ranges. Then Þingvellir National Park, where two tectonic plates meet — you can literally see the gap. Then Brúarárfoss, a gorgeous waterfall. Then the original Geysir — the one that gave its name to all geysers on Earth — with other active geysers next to it, really cool to watch. We finished the day at Gullfoss, a massive, beautiful waterfall.

With the sky still clear, we went aurora hunting again at night. After driving for a while we pulled onto a small road, found her, and just stood there watching the show. Slept at Gesthús Selfoss.

  • Mosfellsbær viewpoint
  • Þingvellir National Park (tectonic plates)
  • Brúarárfoss waterfall
  • Original Geysir + Strokkur geyser
  • Gullfoss waterfall
  • Aurora chasing — and finding her — at night
  • Gesthús Selfoss campsite
★ Save the Golden Circle for a clear day if you can — it makes a huge difference. And keep aurora hunting until your last night; some of the best displays happen unexpectedly.
Day 08

Reykjavík → Home

Slept in a bit, drove to Reykjavík for souvenirs and last bits of Iceland, then returned the van (super smooth at Rent.is) and headed to the airport — back to Paris, and from there everyone home. And that's how this beautiful trip ended.

  • Souvenir shopping in Reykjavík
  • Van return at Rent.is
  • Flight back to Paris
★ Leave at least half a day for Reykjavík shopping and the van return — rushing the drop-off after a 10-day trip is the worst way to end it.

Practical tips

✈️

Getting there

Fly into Keflavík (KEF), 45 min from Reykjavík. Many European airlines offer direct routes. Icelandair often has good fares with a free stopover option.

🚗

Getting around

Rent a 4WD — mandatory in winter, strongly recommended year-round. The Ring Road (Route 1) is paved. Mountain roads (F-roads) require a true 4WD and are closed in winter. Check road.is daily.

🏨

Where to sleep

Accommodation books out fast. Plan and book in advance, especially for summer. Guesthouses and farm stays (called "Farmhouse Hotels") are charming and often cheaper than hotels. Camping is popular May–September.

💰

Budget

Iceland is expensive. Budget roughly €150–200/day per person for accommodation, car, food, and fuel. Cook your own meals where possible — supermarkets (Bónus, Krónan) are everywhere and save a lot. Eating out is very costly.

🌤️

Best season

Summer (Jun–Aug) = midnight sun, green landscapes, all F-roads open. Winter (Nov–Mar) = Northern Lights, snow, fewer crowds. Spring and autumn are sweet spots — decent weather, lower prices, still a chance at the aurora.

🎒

What to pack

Waterproof everything — jacket, boots, trousers. Layers are key. Weather changes in minutes. Sunscreen in summer (midnight sun burns). Aurora alarm app if you're chasing the lights. Portable charger — you'll use your phone as GPS constantly.
